# PRD: Gavel — Online Auction Platform (estate and equipment)

**Sample business:** "Harlan & Pike Auctioneers," a fictional regional house that runs timed online auctions for estates and used equipment (3 synthetic sales, 36 lots, 40 bidders, 220 bid submissions in the frozen fixtures)
**Build log:** #17 Gavel, main track. Chips: high contention · proxy bidding · soft close · auction settlement
**Status:** Draft v1.1 — auctioneer + database-engineer review folded in (see `docs/decisions.md` §Review log)
**Learning objectives:** many writers fighting over one row in the final seconds, far beyond Bookable's two-user race test, with the arbitration rule chosen and justified (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` under READ COMMITTED, no retry loop) and proven by a deterministic barrier test and a measured load script; proxy (max) bidding with the increment table as data; soft-close anti-sniping on an injected clock with a hard cap; an append-only bid ledger that every rejected attempt also lands in; and settlement (winner, second-price-plus-increment, buyer's premium in integer cents) as a pure function the verifier recomputes from the ledger

**Positioning note:** Bookable proved that two concurrent writers cannot double-book a slot, and it did it with an exclusion constraint and two raw connections. Gavel's question is different: not "can two writers collide on an empty slot" but "when fifty writers hit one row in the same second, does every one of them get the right answer, in an order the ledger can show, with nothing lost and nothing counted twice". The row is the lot's state; the order is the bid sequence; the answer depends on every bid before it. That is a serialization problem, not a uniqueness problem, and it is the one thing in the portfolio no sibling has built. `docs/portfolio-overlap.md` lists what is ported, from where, and what is new.

`docs/decisions.md` outranks this document wherever they differ.

Contention numbers (bids per second, latency percentiles) are measured on named hardware by a script in this repo and are not a capacity claim; a number without its hardware and its command is a guess. ## Problem Statement

A timed online auction is quiet for six days and violent for the last ninety seconds. Every bidder who cares is watching one lot, and when the clock shows 0:05 they all press the button. The server sees fifty writes to one row inside a second, each of which must be judged against the state left by the one before it: is this bid above the current minimum, does it beat the leader's hidden maximum, does it move the price, does it push the close out, and who is winning now. Get the order wrong and two people are told they lead; lose a write and someone who bid on time is told they did not; count a retry twice and a bidder is bidding against themselves; read the clock from the client and the auction never ends.

The demo version of this system works: a web form, an `UPDATE lots SET price = …`, a timestamp from the browser. It fails in production in ways that produce a phone call from a lawyer, because an auction is a contract formed under rules the house published, and "the server was busy" is not one of the rules.

Gavel builds the hot path properly and proves it. The bidding rules are a pure engine written from a brief and checked against a Python oracle on every golden and every fixture. The database holds one state row per lot, locked for the duration of each bid, and an append-only ledger where every attempt (accepted or rejected) is a numbered row carrying the state before and after it. Time comes from an injected clock, read after the lock is taken. A client idempotency key makes a retry a replay, not a second bid. A verifier folds the ledger and refuses to let the state row, the sequence or any settlement disagree with it. A barrier test releases N writers at once against a lot in its last second and checks the invariants in SQL. A load script prints bids per second and p95 latency with the hardware it ran on. The builder lesson: contention is a correctness problem before it is a performance problem, and the proof is a ledger you can read, not a number you can quote.

## Goals

1. **The bid rules are data and a pure function.** Increments, soft-close window and cap, and buyer's premium live in `rules/auction.v1.json` (content-hashed) and per-auction settings; `src/auction/engine/engine.ts` turns (lot, state, bid, now) into (outcome, new state) with no database, clock or randomness, and agrees with `tools/auctionlib.py` on every golden (GB-01..GB-23) and every one of the 220 fixture submissions.
2. **One row, one lock, no lost or doubled bids.** A bid is judged under `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the lot's state row at READ COMMITTED; every well-formed attempt writes exactly one ledger row with a gap-free sequence; a client idempotency key makes a retry return the original outcome. The invariants are SQL and run in a barrier test with N writers released together and in `auction:verify` in CI.
3. **Soft close cannot be gamed by the clock.** `now` is injected, read after the lock is held, never taken from the client; a competitive bid extends the close to `now + W`, never past `scheduledEnd + cap`; a bid at or after the close is refused and recorded.
4. **Settlement is recomputable.** Winner, hammer price (second price plus one increment, capped at the winner's max, floored at the reserve), buyer's premium (integer cents, one half-up rounding) and the unsold outcomes are a pure `settle()`; the stored `Settlement` row is insert-only and `auction:verify` recomputes it from the ledger.
5. **Contention is measured, not claimed.** `scripts/load-bids.ts` prints bids per second, p50/p95/p99 latency, lock wait, the hardware and the Postgres version into `docs/LOAD.md`; no write-up quotes a throughput number without that block.
6. **(Builder goal)** Be able to explain, with the ledger, why any bidder saw what they saw at any second of the sale.

## Non-Goals

- **Payments.** No card, ACH or payment provider; settlement ends at a mock invoice (`totalCents`), never at money moving. BoxLoop owns the mock provider pattern; LoanBook owns the ledger-of-money lesson.
- **Real-time push.** The bidder screen polls (`GET` with an ETag, 1–2 s). WebSockets, SSE and presence are Showcall Live's. Polling is a seam, not a shortcut: the engine and the lock are the lesson.
- **Fraud, KYC, shill detection, bidder vetting.** A registered bidder is trusted; a consignor is a text field on the lot. Seam: a `Bidder.status` column and a hook before `placeBid`.
- **Live (in-person) auctioneer clerking, phone and absentee bids, choice lots, buy-now, Dutch or sealed-bid formats.** Timed online lots with proxy bidding only.
- **Catalog media, condition reports, shipping, tax, multi-currency.** A lot has a title, a description and an image URL.
- **A general rules engine.** The rule file has a closed set of keys; a new rule needs a decision and a golden.
- **Multi-process clock agreement.** One app process in this project; the seam is noted (D-15).

## Requirements — Must-Have (P0)

**P0-1: Rules as data and the pure engine** *(core learning artifact #1)*
- [ ] `rules/auction.v1.json`: increment bands in cents, defaults for soft-close window, max extension and buyer's premium (bps); content-hashed; the hash recorded in `docs/decisions.md` and printed by `auction:eval`
- [ ] `src/auction/engine/` (pure: `engine.ts`, `rules.ts`, `money.ts`, `canonical.ts`): `incrementFor`, `minNextBid`, `applyBid`, `fold`, `settle`, `lotStatus`; integer cents, bps and epoch ms only; `rules:lint` greps `src/auction/engine/` for `prisma`, `Date.now`, `new Date`, `Math.random`, `process.env`, `fetch`
- [ ] GB-01..GB-23 reproduced exactly; `auction:eval` replays `fixtures/submissions.jsonl` through the engine and matches `fixtures/expected_bids.csv` and `expected_lots.csv` column for column

**P0-2: Proxy bidding, ties, reserve** *(core learning artifact #2)*
- [ ] A bid is a maximum; the price shown is the least the leader must pay: start price for the first bid, otherwise `min(newMax, oldMax + increment(oldMax))` when the bid beats the leader, `min(leaderMax, bid + increment(bid))` when it does not; the earlier sequence number wins an equal max
- [ ] Reserve is hidden; once the leading max reaches it the price is at least the reserve; a lot whose leading max is under the reserve closes `UNSOLD_RESERVE_NOT_MET`
- [ ] The leader may raise their own max; the price does not move and the close does not extend (O-6)

**P0-3: Soft close on the injected clock** *(core learning artifact #3)*
- [ ] Open interval `[opensAt, closesAt)`; a bid at `closesAt` is `REJECTED_LOT_CLOSED` and recorded
- [ ] After an accepted competitive bid: `closesAt ← min(now + W, scheduledEnd + cap)` if that is later; extensions chain until the cap and stop there
- [ ] `now` is `@/core/clock`'s `now()` read inside the transaction after the lock is acquired; clients never supply a time

**P0-4: The bid path and the ledger** *(core learning artifact #4)*
- [ ] `placeBid({lotId, bidderId, maxCents, idempotencyKey})` in one transaction: `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on `LotState`, read `now()`, idempotency check, `applyBid`, insert one `Bid` row (seq = bidCount + 1, before/after price, leader and close, outcome), update `LotState`, commit; READ COMMITTED; no retry loop; `lock_timeout` mapped to a retryable error that carries the same idempotency key
- [ ] `Bid` and `Settlement` are append-only by trigger; `(lotId, seq)` and `(lotId, bidderId, idempotencyKey)` unique; a replay returns the stored outcome with `replayed: true`; a different amount under the same key is `IDEMPOTENCY_MISMATCH`
- [ ] `LotState` is a verified projection: `auction:verify` folds every lot's ledger and fails on any difference in price, leader, leader max, leader seq, counts, close or extension count

**P0-5: Contention proof** *(core learning artifact #5)*
- [ ] `tests/contention/barrier.test.ts`: N raw connections (N ∈ {2, 10, 25, 50}) each holding a bid, all provably queued on the lot's row lock before release (pg_locks poll, ported from Bookable), fixed `now` one second before close; afterward: N rows, seq 1..N, exactly one leader, price non-decreasing by seq, `LotState` equals the fold, close extended once and only by the first competitive bid, every key once; the same N each retried with their key → still N rows
- [ ] `scripts/load-bids.ts`: M writers over L lots in the last 10 s on a real clock, prints accepted/rejected, bids per second, p50/p95/p99 ms, max lock wait, `os.cpus()[0].model`, core count, memory, Postgres `version()`; writes `docs/LOAD.md`; then `auction:verify` is clean
- [ ] CI runs the barrier test at N = 25 (O-10) and `auction:verify`

**P0-6: Settlement** *(the recomputable claim)*
- [ ] `auction:close -- --as-of <iso>` writes one `Settlement` per closed, unsettled lot: `SOLD` (winner, winning seq, hammer, premium = roundHalfUp(hammer × bps / 10000), total), `UNSOLD_NO_BIDS`, `UNSOLD_RESERVE_NOT_MET`; idempotent per lot
- [ ] `auction:verify` recomputes `settle(fold(ledger))` for every settled lot and fails on any field; a settled lot with later bids is a failure
- [ ] Buyer invoice view: Σ totals per bidder per auction, integer cents, no payment

**P0-7: Verify and eval** *(the gate)*
- [ ] `npm run auction:verify`: the SQL invariants in brief §8 over every lot; non-zero exit with the first failing lot and check named
- [ ] `npm run auction:eval`: engine replay of the fixtures (no database) and a database replay (sequential `placeBid` with a scripted clock) both diffed against `expected_bids.csv` and `expected_lots.csv`; prints the brief §10 table

**P0-8: Minimal surfaces (Phase 3)**
- [ ] Staff: create auction, lots, publish; lot history (every row, in seq order). Bidder: register (paddle), lot page with current price, next minimum, "you lead / outbid", close countdown from the server's `closesAt`, bid form with a client-generated idempotency key; polling with ETag; outbid and won notices through the outbox

## Risks

- **The projection drifts from the ledger.** A bug in `placeBid` writes a state the fold would not. Mitigation: `auction:verify` in CI and after every contention test; the barrier test asserts the fold, not just the counts.
- **Lock convoy.** Fifty writers queue behind one lock and the last waits the sum of the others. Mitigation: measured, not hidden; the hold time is one engine call and two writes; `lock_timeout` turns a long wait into a retryable error, and the load script prints the max wait. Not mitigated by reading the state outside the lock, which would reintroduce the race.
- **Clock read before the lock.** A bid that waited 2 s behind the lock would carry a stale time and be accepted after close. Mitigation: D-15, `now()` after `FOR UPDATE`, and verify checks `placedAt` is non-decreasing by seq.
- **Idempotency key reuse across lots or bidders.** Mitigation: the key is scoped `(lotId, bidderId, key)`; the fixtures include the same string used by two bidders.
- **The oracle encodes the author's conventions.** Mitigation: every rule is in `docs/decisions.md` with a default and an O-item where a house might differ; Lena's lens in the review log.
- **Pool exhaustion in tests.** The barrier test's N raw connections plus Prisma's pool of 10 must stay under Postgres's `max_connections`. Mitigation: N ≤ 50 and the raw clients are closed in `finally`.
